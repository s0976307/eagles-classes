TEST SHEET: GEORGE W. BUSH (43rd President, 2001-2009)
Source: Miller Center essays (summaries). Verify key facts against the originals before the test.

KEY DATES
- Jul 6, 1946: born, New Haven, CT. Grew up in Midland, TX.
- 1968: Yale graduate. Texas Air National Guard. Harvard MBA.
- Nov 5, 1977: married Laura Welch. Twins Barbara and Jenna born 1981.
- 1978: lost U.S. House race.
- 1994: defeated Gov. Ann Richards. 1998: reelected Texas governor (69%).
- 1999: bought Crawford, TX ranch.
- 2000: election; lost popular vote to Gore; Bush v. Gore (5-4, Dec 2000); Electoral College 271-266.
- Jan 8, 2002: No Child Left Behind signed.
- Sep 11, 2001: terrorist attacks (nearly 3,000 killed).
- Oct 7, 2001: air strikes in Afghanistan begin; Kandahar falls Dec 7, 2001.
- Sep 2002: National Security Strategy (Bush Doctrine: preventive war).
- Mar 19, 2003: Iraq invasion begins. Mar 2003 onward: no WMDs found.
- May 1, 2003: "Mission Accomplished" speech, USS Abraham Lincoln.
- Dec 8, 2003: Medicare Modernization Act (prescription drugs).
- 2004: reelected over John Kerry (286-251; 50.73%).
- Aug 29, 2005: Hurricane Katrina; "Brownie, you're doing a heck of a job."
- 2005: John Roberts chief justice; Samuel Alito confirmed.
- Jan 2007: Petraeus surge (about 20,000 troops).
- 2006: Republicans lose both houses of Congress.
- Sep-Oct 2008: Lehman collapse; TARP ($700 billion).
- 2008: Republicans lose the White House. Left office at 62 with 33% approval.

MAIN IDEAS
- "Compassionate conservatism": faith-based initiatives, education reform, Medicare drug benefit.
- Two tax cuts ($1.35 trillion total) that were temporary; deficits grew; they reduced revenue by about $4 trillion over ten years (essay estimate).
- Stem cell policy (Aug 2001): federal funds only for existing embryonic stem cell lines.
- Post-9/11 expanded presidential power; about 90% approval after the attacks.
- Surveillance and detention: Guantanamo; enhanced interrogation; NSA warrantless surveillance; Patriot Act; Hamdan v. Rumsfeld (2006) rejected the military tribunals.
- Foreign aid: PEPFAR (HIV/AIDS in Africa), Millennium Challenge Account, President's Malaria Initiative (2005).
- Failed reforms: Social Security partial privatization (dropped 2005); immigration reform.
- Polarization: Democrats questioned his legitimacy; Jeffords switched parties May 2001 (51-49 Senate).
- Legacy: "lightning rod for controversy" (Miller Center); NCLB replaced in 2015; longest U.S. war (Afghanistan); Iraq deaths and insurrection.

LIKELY QUESTIONS
- Why was 2000 controversial? Lost the popular vote; Florida recount; Supreme Court ruled 5-4 in Bush v. Gore.
- What was the Bush Doctrine? Preventive war, unilateral action, spreading democracy (2002).
- Two main policy results of 9/11? Afghanistan war; Iraq war (2003) and Patriot Act.
- Why did his approval fall? Iraq (no WMDs), Katrina, the economy and 2008 crash.
- What domestic reform bridged parties? No Child Left Behind (2002), with Ted Kennedy.
- What did Medicare reform add? Prescription drug benefits (2003), largest expansion since 1965.
