BARACK OBAMA (44th President)
Overview author: Michael Nelson (Fulmer Professor, Rhodes College; Miller Center senior fellow).
Chicago citation: Miller Center of Public Affairs, University of Virginia. "Barack Obama." https://millercenter.org/president/obama
==================================================================

LIFE IN BRIEF (https://millercenter.org/president/obama/life-in-brief)
- Inaugurated January 20, 2009, as the 44th president; first African American president.
- Born to a white American mother and a Kenyan father; grew up in Hawaii.
- Degrees from Columbia and Harvard Law School; Chicago community organizer.
- Married Michelle Robinson in 1992; two daughters.
- Eight years in the Illinois state senate; U.S. Senate from 2004.
- Announced candidacy February 2007; beat Hillary Clinton in a close primary; beat John McCain in the general election.
- Took office during a recession with the financial "bail-out" inherited from Bush and troops in Iraq and Afghanistan.
- First term: economic recovery, health care reform, most troops out of Iraq. Republicans took the House in 2010.
- Second term: immigration and gun control stalled. Republicans took the Senate in 2014; more executive action.
- Foreign policy in the second term focused on the Middle East and climate change.
- Left office at age 55; planned to stay in Washington while his younger daughter finished high school; helped build the Obama Presidential Center in Chicago.

LIFE BEFORE THE PRESIDENCY (https://millercenter.org/president/obama/life-before-the-presidency)
- Born in Hawaii in 1961 to Ann Dunham and Barack Obama Sr.; father left when he was two.
- Ages 6-10 in Indonesia with mother and stepfather; returned to Hawaii to live with grandparents; attended Punahou School.
- Occidental College, then Columbia (graduated 1983). Community organizer on Chicago's South Side (Altgeld Gardens); this convinced him he needed a law degree.
- Harvard Law (entered 1988); first African American elected president of the Harvard Law Review.
- Memoir Dreams from My Father (1995). Met Michelle Robinson while interning at a Chicago law firm; married 1992.
- Civil rights attorney; taught at the University of Chicago Law School.
- 1996: won an Illinois state senate seat after contesting the petitions of rival Alice Palmer.
- 2000: lost a primary to U.S. Representative Bobby Rush.
- 2002: opposed the Iraq War in a speech; 2004 Senate win after the Republican opponent withdrew.
- 2004 Democratic National Convention keynote brought national attention. "The audacity of hope" phrase (from Jeremiah Wright; book 2006).

CAMPAIGNS AND ELECTIONS (https://millercenter.org/president/obama/campaigns-and-elections)
- 2008: launched early 2007 with grassroots fundraising; narrowly beat Hillary Clinton for the nomination; opposition to Iraq War helped. Chose Joe Biden as running mate; steered through the September 2008 financial crisis; beat McCain.
- 2010 midterms: Republicans opposed most initiatives; GOP gained the House. Lame-duck deals: tax cuts, unemployment benefits, repeal of "don't ask, don't tell," New START.
- 2012: weak economy; beat Mitt Romney with a smaller margin than 2008; Democrats gained Senate seats.
- 2014 midterms: low turnout; Republicans gained the Senate; Obama signaled reliance on executive action.
- 2016 postscript: campaigned for Clinton; called Trump "temperamentally unfit to be president"; after Trump won, Obama pledged to help him succeed.

DOMESTIC AFFAIRS (https://millercenter.org/president/obama/domestic-affairs)
- Transition: about 11 weeks; Rahm Emanuel as chief of staff. Cabinet of rivals ("team of rivals"), including Hillary Clinton and Republican Robert Gates. William Daley became chief of staff after 2010.
- Economy: TARP bank rescue; auto industry aid; $800 billion stimulus passed with no Republican votes. Recovery by the end of his presidency, though growth was modest and inequality persisted.
- Health care: budget-neutral plan. Affordable Care Act signed March 2010; survived repeal efforts and a 2012 Supreme Court challenge. Troubled website launch. Uninsured rate fell.
- Other first-term laws: Lilly Ledbetter Fair Pay Act; Race to the Top; Dodd-Frank (created the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau).
- Budget fights: grand bargain with Speaker Boehner collapsed; "super committee" failed; sequestration; 2013 shutdown. Deficit shrank substantially.
- Judges: Sonia Sotomayor and Elena Kagan appointed. Senate Republicans refused to consider Merrick Garland (2016). Democrats changed rules so simple majorities confirm most nominees.
- Second term: John Kerry replaced Clinton at State. Immigration reform, gun control, and minimum wage proposals stalled; immigration bill passed the Senate but died in the House.
- Executive action: climate regulations, federal contractor minimum wage order, LGBT worker protections. Vetoed 12 bills; Congress overrode one. Far more pardons and commutations than the previous 13 presidents combined, mostly drug offenders.

FOREIGN AFFAIRS (https://millercenter.org/president/obama/foreign-affairs)
- Opposed the 2003 Iraq invasion; troop levels fell; about 150 troops remained in Iraq by 2012.
- Afghanistan: added troops, then a 2009 surge with withdrawal planned for 2011; about 12,000 troops by 2015.
- May 2011: Navy SEAL team killed Osama bin Laden.
- Drone and special forces expansion. NATO action in Libya; Benghazi attack (four Americans killed) was a major consequence.
- Syria: declined missile strikes after chemical weapons use; accepted Russian help removing Syria's chemical arsenal.
- ISIS (2014): air strikes in Iraq and Syria; argued the 2001 authorization covered it.
- 2015 Iran nuclear agreement: limit on Iran's nuclear program for at least a decade in exchange for lifting UN sanctions.
- Restored relations with Cuba; climate agreement with China (groundwork for 2015 Paris conference); Trans-Pacific Partnership never submitted to Congress.
- Approach: pragmatic, case-by-case; "Don't do stupid stuff."

FAMILY LIFE (https://millercenter.org/president/obama/family-life)
- Opened with Obama's view of diversity, tied to his multicultural family.
- Married Michelle Robinson in 1992; daughters born in Chicago. Moved to Washington in January 2009; daughters attended Sidwell Friends. Michelle's mother lived in the White House for eight years.
- Extended family: Craig Robinson (Michelle's brother); half-sister Maya Soetoro Ng.
- Mother shaped his empathy and political views.
- After office: stayed in Washington while Sasha finished high school; Malia to attend Harvard; family bought a home in Kalorama.
- Said he would stay mostly out of day-to-day politics but speak up when core values were at stake. Criticized successor's travel ban shortly after leaving office.

IMPACT AND LEGACY (https://millercenter.org/president/obama/impact-and-legacy)
- First African American president; a notably stable administration.
- Key achievements (Affordable Care Act, Paris accord) were overturned or challenged once Republicans controlled the White House and Congress.
- Nelson: his greatest impact may be what did not happen. The economy avoided "the abyss of a second Great Depression in 2009." Fewer new wars.
- Policies based on unilateral executive action are most vulnerable to reversal.
- Approval reached about 60% near the end of his term.
- C-SPAN survey of 91 presidential scholars: 12th overall (above Bush, Clinton, and George H.W. Bush; below Reagan at 8th).

DONALD TRUMP (45th and 47th President)
Overview author: Benjamin C. Waterhouse (Professor of History, UNC Chapel Hill).
Chicago citation: Miller Center of Public Affairs, University of Virginia. "Donald Trump." https://millercenter.org/president/trump
==================================================================

LIFE IN BRIEF (https://millercenter.org/president/trump/life-in-brief)
- Won November 8, 2016 (an upset); inaugurated January 20, 2017, as the 45th president. First term dominated by scandal, investigations, and partisan division.
- Lost the 2020 election to Joe Biden.
- Won a second, non-consecutive term in 2024; sworn in again January 20, 2025 (47th president).
- Oldest person to take office in 2016 (age 70); first president with no prior public office or military leadership.
- 2016: lost the popular vote by about 2.8 million; won the Electoral College 304-227. 2024: won the Electoral College 312-226 and the popular vote over Kamala Harris.
- Two impeachments: December 2019 (Ukraine) and January 2021 (Capitol attack). Acquitted both times.
- Refused to concede 2020; January 6, 2021 Capitol attack.
- May 2024: convicted in New York on 34 felony counts of falsifying business records; first president convicted of a felony. Unconditional discharge; announced an appeal.
- Born June 14, 1946, in Queens; ran his father's real estate business; hotels, casinos, golf courses; star of The Apprentice.

CAMPAIGNS AND ELECTIONS (https://millercenter.org/president/trump/campaigns-and-elections)
- First president elected without prior public service experience.
- Hinted at runs in 1987, 1999 (joined the Reform Party), and 2012. Launched 2016 campaign June 16, 2015, at Trump Tower.
- 2016 message: "Make America Great Again"; immigration restriction; criticism of free trade; "America First."
- Beat Ted Cruz and John Kasich for the nomination; running mate Mike Pence.
- Late 2016 issues: Access Hollywood tape; Russian hacking reports; refusal to release tax returns.
- 2020: "Keep America Great"; COVID-19 reshaped campaigning. Biden won 306-232 and by over 7 million votes. Trump refused to concede and pressured Pence and state officials to block certification. January 6, 2021 Capitol attack.
- 2024: Senate acquitted him in the second impeachment trial 57-43. Supreme Court unanimously blocked state attempts to bar him from the ballot under the Fourteenth Amendment. Biden withdrew; Harris became the nominee. Trump won November 5, 2024, by roughly 2.5 million votes; running mate JD Vance.

DOMESTIC AFFAIRS (https://millercenter.org/president/trump/domestic-affairs)
- Priorities: restrict immigration; infrastructure; cut taxes; repeal the Affordable Care Act. Results mixed.
- Personnel: high turnover. Michael Flynn fired after three weeks; Rex Tillerson and Jim Mattis also left.
- Russia investigation: fired FBI Director James Comey (May 2017). Mueller found "numerous links" between Russia and the campaign but no clear conspiracy; did not determine obstruction.
- ACA: Senate repeal failed (July 2017). December 2017 tax law eliminated the individual mandate penalty.
- Tax Cuts and Jobs Act (December 2017): biggest benefits to high-income Americans. Nearly 100 regulations suspended or revoked early on.
- Immigration: 2017 travel ban on seven mostly Muslim countries. 35-day shutdown ended in 2019 with $1.375 billion for border barriers. 2018 family separation policy reversed by executive order June 2018.
- COVID-19: CARES Act (March 2020) $2.2 trillion. By April 2020 the U.S. had more deaths than any other country.
- 2017 Charlottesville rally turned violent. 2020 protests after George Floyd's murder; Trump called for "law and order."
- Supreme Court: Neil Gorsuch (2017, 54-45), Brett Kavanaugh (2018, 50-48), Amy Coney Barrett (2020).
- Impeachments: December 2019 (abuse of power, obstruction of Congress), acquitted February 2020. January 2021 (incitement of insurrection), acquitted February 2021 with 57 senators voting guilty.

FOREIGN AFFAIRS (https://millercenter.org/president/trump/foreign-affairs)
- "America First": isolationist-leaning strand marginalized since WWII.
- April 2017: cruise missile strikes on a Syrian airfield. December 2018: announced withdrawal of about 2,000 troops from Syria; Mattis resigned.
- February 2020 Taliban deal led to full withdrawal in 2021; Taliban retook Afghanistan September 2021.
- Withdrew from the Paris Agreement (June 2017), the Iran nuclear deal (May 2018), and the WHO. Threatened to leave NATO. Biden reversed Paris and WHO.
- December 2017: recognized Jerusalem as Israel's capital; embassy opened May 2018. 2020 Abraham Accords (Israel-UAE, Israel-Bahrain), brokered with Jared Kushner.
- Trade: withdrew from TPP; replaced NAFTA with USMCA (2018); China tariffs from January 2018; trade war eased after a 2019 agreement.
- North Korea: "fire and fury," then Singapore summit with Kim Jong-un (June 2018); first sitting president to enter North Korea (2019). No denuclearization.
- Russia: praised Putin during the campaign. Mueller indicted six Trump associates and twelve Russians. Accepted Putin's denial of election interference (2018).
- Second term: Russia's 2022 invasion of Ukraine; Hamas's October 2023 attack and the Gaza war; fall of Bashar al-Assad (December 2024). Picks: Marco Rubio (State), Pete Hegseth (Defense), Tulsi Gabbard (DNI), prioritizing loyalty.

LIFE BETWEEN THE PRESIDENCIES (https://millercenter.org/president/trump/life-between-the-presidencies)
- Lost 2020 by about 7 million popular votes; 232-306 in the Electoral College. Claimed the election was "stolen."
- January 6, 2021: rally near the White House, then the Capitol attack; he declined to call off the riot.
- Second impeachment: Senate convicted 57-43, ten votes short of two-thirds; remained eligible to run.
- Skipped Biden's January 20, 2021 inauguration; first outgoing president to do so since Nixon.
- Post-presidency: Mar-a-Lago; alternative social media; fundraising; 2022 midterm campaigning.
- Announced 2024 candidacy November 2022; nominated July 2024; won November 2024; re-inaugurated January 20, 2025.
- Sentencing: unconditional discharge (no jail, fines, or probation); announced appeal.

FAMILY LIFE (https://millercenter.org/president/trump/family-life)
- Married three times; divorced twice; the only president with that record.
- First marriage: Ivana Zelnickova (1977); children Donald Jr., Ivanka, Eric; divorced 1992.
- Second marriage: Marla Maples (1993); daughter Tiffany; divorced 1999.
- Third marriage: Melania Knauss (January 2005); son Barron (2006).
- Residences: Trump Tower until the final months of his presidency; Mar-a-Lago (bought 1985, "Winter White House"); legal domicile changed to Florida October 2019.
- Donald Jr. and Eric ran the Trump Organization; Ivanka and Jared Kushner were White House advisers (a Justice Department opinion said the 1967 "Robert Kennedy Law" did not apply).
- Brother Fred Trump Jr. died of alcoholism in 1981 at 43; Trump cites this for avoiding alcohol.
- Niece Mary L. Trump's 2020 critical book, Too Much and Never Enough.

IMPACT AND LEGACY (https://millercenter.org/president/trump/impact-and-legacy)
- First American since Grover Cleveland to win non-consecutive terms; legacy still developing.
- Shaped the Republican Party: tension between big business/trade and cultural/populist wings.
- Broke with past practice: distanced the U.S. from alliances, praised autocrats, declined to disavow white supremacist groups, hardline immigration stance.
- Norm violations: did not release tax returns (every president since Ford had); kept businesses under adult sons instead of a blind trust.
- Fired Comey; refused to cooperate with Mueller.
- Most serious norm violation: refusal to accept the 2020 loss, which fueled January 6.
- Second term began with Republican congressional majorities and loyalist advisers.

JOE BIDEN (46th President)
Overview: Miller Center lists Barbara A. Perry as the associated professor; the page does not explicitly name her as author of the essays.
Chicago citation: Miller Center of Public Affairs, University of Virginia. "Joe Biden." https://millercenter.org/president/biden
==================================================================

LIFE IN BRIEF (https://millercenter.org/president/biden/life-in-brief)
- Born November 20, 1942, in Scranton, PA; working-class Irish-Catholic family. Overcame a severe childhood stutter.
- University of Delaware (1965); Syracuse Law (1968). Public defender in Wilmington, DE.
- 1972: at 29, unexpectedly defeated Republican Senator J. Caleb Boggs. Weeks later a car crash killed his first wife, Neilia, and infant daughter, Naomi; sons Beau and Hunter were seriously injured.
- 36 years in the Senate; Judiciary and Foreign Relations committees; chaired confirmation hearings for five Supreme Court justices, including Bork and Thomas.
- Failed presidential bids (1988, 2008). Obama chose him as 2008 running mate; eight years as vice president.
- 2020: beat Trump 306-232; running mate Kamala Harris. Took office at 78, the oldest president in U.S. history.
- Presidency: COVID-19 pandemic; bipartisan infrastructure bill; Ketanji Brown Jackson nominated to the Supreme Court; rejoined WHO and Paris Agreement.
- 2024: poor debate performance; withdrew and endorsed Harris. Trump won the 2024 election.

LIFE BEFORE THE PRESIDENCY (https://millercenter.org/president/biden/life-before-the-presidency)
- Father later sold cars in Wilmington after the family moved there in 1953.
- Archmere Academy bullying; classmates called him "Dash." Sports built confidence ("Hands").
- Syracuse Law (1968); briefly at a corporate firm, then public defender.
- 1970: New Castle County Council. 1972: beat Boggs by fewer than 3,000 votes.
- December 1972: wife Neilia and daughter Naomi ("Amy") killed in a crash while Christmas shopping. Beau and Hunter injured.
- Married Jill Jacobs in 1977; daughter Ashley born 1981.
- Senate: 17 years on Judiciary, 11 on Foreign Relations. Bork (1987) and Thomas (1991) hearings; Thomas hearings criticized over Anita Hill.
- Backed the 1994 crime bill; introduced the Violence Against Women Act (1990; passed 1994). Voted for the 2002 Iraq War, later called a "mistake."

CAMPAIGNS AND ELECTIONS (https://millercenter.org/president/biden/campaigns-and-elections)
- 1988: entered June 9, 1987; dropped out September 23 after plagiarism reports (borrowed lines from Neil Kinnock). Two brain aneurysms that year, repaired.
- 2008: launched January 31, 2007; "articulate and bright and clean" remark; weak Iowa showing (January 3, 2008) ended the bid.
- 2020: announced April 25, 2019; "battle for the soul of this nation." Fourth in Iowa, fifth in New Hampshire; won South Carolina with 48.6% (Clyburn endorsement); Sanders withdrew April 8.
- Running mate Kamala Harris. Mostly virtual convention; acceptance speech in Wilmington.
- Result: called November 7, 2020. Electoral College 306-232; about 81 million votes to Trump's 74 million. Turnout 66.2%, highest in 120 years.
- January 6, 2021 attack tied to Trump's false fraud claims.
- 2024: announced April 25, 2023; nomination secured March 12, 2024. June 27, 2024 debate; withdrew July 21; endorsed Harris (running mate Tim Walz), who lost by a little over 2 million votes.

DOMESTIC AFFAIRS (https://millercenter.org/president/biden/domestic-affairs)
- Took office January 20, 2021; focus on COVID and the economy.
- Diverse cabinet: Janet Yellen (first female Treasury secretary); Xavier Becerra (first Latino HHS secretary); Deb Haaland (first Indigenous cabinet secretary, Interior).
- Rehired Obama officials (Vilsack, McDonough); named Merrick Garland attorney general.
- American Rescue Plan Act (signed March 11, 2021). Infrastructure law (November 2021). Inflation reached 7% in his first year.
- August 2022: CHIPS and Science Act ($280 billion for semiconductors); Inflation Reduction Act (lower drug prices; clean energy).
- Immigration: southern border encounters nearly doubled to more than 2 million a year. 2024 bipartisan border bill failed after Trump urged Republicans to oppose it.
- Dobbs (2022) overturned Roe v. Wade; affirmative action struck down in college admissions. Ketanji Brown Jackson first Black woman on the Court (2022). Respect for Marriage Act; Juneteenth holiday law.
- After Uvalde (May 2022): Bipartisan Safer Communities Act, first major gun law in three decades.

FOREIGN AFFAIRS (https://millercenter.org/president/biden/foreign-affairs)
- Day one: returned the U.S. to the Paris Agreement.
- Antony Blinken (State); Lloyd Austin (first Black Defense secretary); Avril Haines (first woman DNI).
- China: met Xi in San Francisco (late 2022); tensions after a Chinese balloon was shot down (2023).
- Afghanistan: chaotic August 2021 withdrawal; Taliban took Kabul; airport bombing killed 13 U.S. service members. Approval fell from 57% to 40%.
- Russia-Ukraine: U.S. led 50+ allies in aid and sanctions; no U.S. troops. About $70 billion in military aid by early 2025.
- October 7, 2023 Hamas attack: at least 1,200 killed (46 Americans), about 250 hostages. Biden backed Israel's defense while urging fewer civilian casualties.
- Spring 2024: $17 billion for Israel in a foreign aid bill; $20 billion fighter jet sale.
- Blinken's shuttle diplomacy for ceasefires and hostage deals; Gaza humanitarian pier. Perceived ineffectiveness in both wars contributed to his withdrawal.

LIFE AFTER THE PRESIDENCY (https://millercenter.org/president/biden/life-after-the-presidency)
- Not retrieved (HTTP 404).

FAMILY LIFE (https://millercenter.org/president/biden/family-life)
- Met Neilia Hunter on a spring break trip to Nassau (1964-1966); married 1966. Children: Beau (1969), Hunter (1970), Naomi "Amy" (1971).
- 1972 crash: a tractor-trailer hit the car Neilia drove with the children; Neilia and Amy died; Beau and Hunter injured.
- Mike Mansfield and Ted Kennedy urged him to take his Senate seat; commuted by train ("Amtrak Joe").
- Met Jill Jacobs in 1975; married 1977; she helped raise Beau and Hunter; Ashley born 1981.
- Beau died of brain cancer in May 2015. Biden announced October 21, 2015 he would not run in 2016.
- Hunter's legal troubles: 2023 federal gun conviction; September 2024 guilty plea on tax charges (about $1.4 million owed).
- December 1, 2024: full pardon for Hunter for federal offenses from 2014 to 2024.

IMPACT AND LEGACY (https://millercenter.org/president/biden/impact-and-legacy)
- Essay by Barbara A. Perry: hard to predict how history will judge Biden so soon after office.
- Priorities: civil rights for minorities and women; affordable health insurance; reducing inequality.
- Calmed the country after January 6; managed COVID.
- Credited with restoring U.S. world leadership, prioritizing a cleaner environment, and high-tech industrial investment.
- Student loan debt relief plan voided by the Supreme Court.
- Post-pandemic spending fueled inflation; setbacks in Afghanistan, Ukraine, and Israel.
- Argues Biden's delay in leaving the 2024 race hurt Democrats.
- Unlike Truman, Carter, and both Bushes, Biden (then 82, facing a serious cancer diagnosis) will not have years to rebuild his reputation.
