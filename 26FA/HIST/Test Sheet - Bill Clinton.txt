TEST SHEET: BILL CLINTON (42nd President, 1993-2001)
Source: Miller Center essays (summaries). Verify key facts against the originals before the test.

KEY DATES
- Aug 19, 1946: born, Hope, AR (after father's death). Stepfather Roger Clinton; moved to Hot Springs (1950).
- Jul 1963: met President Kennedy (Boys Nation).
- 1964: entered Georgetown. Rhodes Scholar to Oxford (no degree). 1973: Yale Law.
- 1974: lost U.S. House race.
- 1975: married Hillary Rodham.
- 1976: Arkansas attorney general. 1978: governor at age 32.
- 1980: lost reelection; 1982: returned as governor.
- 1992: defeated George H.W. Bush and Ross Perot (19% popular vote, no electoral votes).
- 1993: NAFTA passed; economic package passed with no Republican votes.
- 1993: Motor Voter law; Ruth Bader Ginsburg appointed to the Supreme Court.
- Oct 1993: Somalia (U.S. soldiers dragged through Mogadishu); withdrew by March 1994.
- 1993-1994: health care plan (led by Hillary) fails; "Harry and Louise" ads.
- Apr 1994: Rwanda genocide (about 800,000 killed); Clinton apologized in 1998.
- 1994: Republicans win Congress (first since 1955); Contract with America.
- 1995: Dayton Accords (Bosnia); about 20,000 U.S. troops.
- 1996: welfare reform ("end welfare as we know it"); reelected over Bob Dole.
- May 27, 1997: Clinton v. Jones (9-0): Paula Jones's suit can proceed.
- Aug 17, 1998: admits "inappropriate" conduct with Monica Lewinsky.
- Dec 1998: House impeaches; Feb 12, 1999: Senate acquits.
- 1999: Kosovo; NATO bombing ends Serbian ethnic cleansing.
- 1998 midterms: Republicans lose five House seats.

MAIN IDEAS
- "New Democrat" approach: middle-class focus, fiscal discipline, free trade, welfare reform, crime policy.
- Economy: 1993 package passed with no Republican votes; deficit fell from $290B (1992) to $203B (1994); surplus of $124B by 1999.
- Doctrine of enlargement: expand market democracies through trade, multilateral peacekeeping, and intervention.
- Foreign wins: Northern Ireland peace (George Mitchell); Dayton (Bosnia); Kosovo; Nunn-Lugar nuclear security in Russia.
- Foreign failures: Somalia, Rwanda (no decisive action), Haiti (negotiated with Carter, Powell, Nunn).
- Health care failure was his biggest domestic defeat.
- Impeachment: second president impeached; Senate acquittal; he kept his job.
- Party impact: Republicans gained Congress; Democrats lost state governorships and legislatures.
- The American franchise: Motor Voter law; Prop 187 and Prop 209 in California; Hopwood v. Texas; Million Man March (1995).

LIKELY QUESTIONS
- Why did Republicans win Congress in 1994? Health care failure; Contract with America (Newt Gingrich).
- What was the "New Democrat" approach? Middle-class focus, welfare reform, fiscal discipline, free trade, crime policy.
- Why did the Senate acquit him? Perjury and obstruction charges not enough for removal (Feb 12, 1999).
- What was the Doctrine of Enlargement? Expand market democracies through trade, peacekeeping, and intervention.
- What were NAFTA and GATT? Free-trade agreements (1993 and 1994).
- What did Clinton's legacy look like? Fiscal surpluses and free trade, but the failed health care plan and the Rwanda failure.
- Which Clinton foreign-policy actions were successful? Dayton Accords (Bosnia) and Kosovo (NATO bombing, 1999).
